# The text reader's drag: "events" at 75 ms a frame, and a flick too strong (2026-11-21)

Owner, handheld, an EPUB dragged in Files' text reader: jittery, every frame `timing: frame 85 ms (events 77, top draw 2)`.

## What a drag costs on the Mac

`swipe:` (`docs/workflow.md`: a move queued every 8 ms, as `touch.cpp` sends them) with `BP_FRAME_TIMING=1`
at `BP_BOTTOM_SIZE=1024x768`, five swipes over a 20-chapter generated EPUB: every pass `events` 0–1 ms, `event
pump` 0, one move a pass. `Ui::pointer` for a text Move is ~0.01 ms (it only queues a `TextReader::Scroll`); the
place is remembered once a pass (`Docs::tick`), written at most every 2 s on the writer's thread. So the handheld's
75 ms is not the app's handling of a move.

## The likely cause (handheld only, not measurable here)

SDL on KMSDRM reads the same touchscreen through evdev and turns each finger move into a mouse move
(`SDL_HINT_TOUCH_MOUSE_EVENTS`), which moves SDL's KMSDRM cursor: `drmModeMoveCursor`, a legacy cursor ioctl that on
an atomic driver commits and can wait for the CRTC's next flip — inside `SDL_PollEvent`, so it lands in "events".
The text reader flips the top panel every frame while dragged, which is what Music's lists (59 a second) don't.
Now, when the app reads the touchscreen itself (`ownTouch`), `main` turns SDL's touch-to-mouse off and hides the
cursor (nothing used SDL's copy of the touch there).

To confirm on the handheld: the frame line now splits `event pump` (SDL_PollEvent's own time) from `events` (the
handling) and gives `event count`. If `event pump` is still high, the cursor wasn't it: look at SDL's evdev.

## Also changed

- A pass's queued finger moves are coalesced: a Move with a later Move already queued is dropped (`evTouch` peek).
- The text reader keeps drawn rows within a screen above and below the view (`TextReader::keepNear`): a drag back
  and forth draws none of them again (a new row is FreeType + an upload: the 32 ms "top draw" frames).
- The flick: `lay::Flick` (scroller.h, test `testTextFlick`): the finger's speed over its last 100 ms; under 0.9
  units/ms, or a finger still 40 ms before the lift, no glide; else 0.6x the speed, slowing by e every 250 ms (a
  list's: 325). The old one glided from 0.1 units/ms at the full speed.
